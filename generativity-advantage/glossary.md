# Glossary: The Generativity Advantage

### Generativity
The capacity of a system to drive unanticipated innovation through the unfiltered contributions of broad, varied, and often uncoordinated audiences (Zittrain's definition).

### Generativity Advantage
The strategic benefit gained by profiting from the unpredicted innovations of others on a massive scale.

### Leverage
The degree to which a system simplifies difficult tasks for its users, magnifying their creative or productive potential.

### Adaptability
The ease with which a system can be modified or reconfigured by users for purposes not explicitly intended by the original designers.

### Ease of Mastery
The relative effort required for new contributors to learn, understand, and effectively utilize the system's capabilities.

### Accessibility
The absence of technical or social barriers to accessing and building upon a technology.

### Transferability
The ease with which modifications and innovations can be shared, disseminated, and built upon by other participants in the ecosystem.

### Profitability (Generative Characteristic)
The ease with which user-innovators can capture economic value from their contributions to the platform.

### Allocation Layer
The foundational layer of a marketplace focused on basic functions: matching buyers/sellers, processing transactions, and ensuring trust and safety.

### Generation Layer
The layer of a marketplace architected to spur innovation through knowledge sharing, toolkits, and structured serendipity.

### Appropriation Layer
The layer of a marketplace designed for value capture and distribution, aligning platform revenue with participant success.

### Boundary Resources
The technical interfaces (APIs, SDKs, No-code tools) that define the rules of engagement and enable external parties to build on top of a platform.

### Control Points
Strategic or technical areas in a value chain where power can be exercised to guide innovation or capture value (e.g., core APIs, data, brand).

### Knowledge Spillovers
The unintended flow of ideas and insights between people and projects that sparks new innovations (treated as a design target in generative systems).

### Adjacent Possible
The space of potential new combinations that could emerge from the current state of a system (Kauffman).

### Structural Serendipity
The intentional design of environments (e.g., forums, discovery tools) that increase the likelihood of valuable accidental encounters and insights.

### Experimentation Engine
A cycle where a platform makes experimentation low-cost, captures knowledge from failures, and broadcasts insights back to the community to accelerate innovation.

### Residual Claimancy
The right of innovators to capture a significant portion of the value they create (essential for aligning incentives in a generative ecosystem).

### Vibe Coding
A term for AI-powered programming where the user describes intent (the "vibe") and the AI generates the implementation, dramatically lowering the "Ease of Mastery" barrier.

### Killer App
A specific application of a generative tool that demonstrates its practical value to the mainstream, often catalyzing mass adoption (e.g., VisiCalc for the PC).
