# Patterns of Generativity

Practical techniques and design principles for building and managing generative systems.

## 1. Designing for Generativity

### Pattern: The Lego Architecture

**Use when:** You want to build a tool that can be repurposed for thousands of use cases.

- **Action:** Break the product into modular, reusable components (e.g., Notion blocks, Bubble elements).
- **Goal:** Allow users to engage in "behavioral composition"—layering simple functions to create complex outcomes.

### Pattern: The Boundary Resource Mix

**Use when:** You need to balance openness with platform integrity.

- **Action:** Provide a tiered set of resources:
  - **APIs/SDKs:** For professional developers.
  - **No-Code/AI Builders:** For non-technical "vibe coders."
  - **Documentation:** Thick documentation that explains _why_ and _how_ rather than just _what_.

### Pattern: The Experimentation Engine

**Use when:** You want the market to do the R&D for you.

- **Action:**
  1. Reduce the cost of failure (e.g., low-fee entry, free sandbox).
  2. Capture data from successes and failures.
  3. Aggregate patterns.
  4. Broadcast "Frontier Knowledge" back to the community to level them up.

## 2. Managing Generativity

### Pattern: The Patient Play

**Use when:** You are launching a highly abstract "meta-product" (like Notion or ChatGPT).

- **Action:** Resist the urge to "pivot" toward the first specific use case that works.
- **Goal:** Maintain strategic ambiguity long enough for the community to discover the product's true versatile potential.

### Pattern: Problem Hunting

**Use when:** Traditional user testing feels too narrow.

- **Action:** Expose the product to diverse populations without preconceived notions. Find the "lead users" who are hacking the tool to solve niche problems.
- **Goal:** Shift from hypothesis testing to hypothesis development.

### Pattern: Transformative Remixing

**Use when:** You want to encourage participation while maintaining quality/originality.

- **Action:** Design "cloning" or "forking" features (e.g., GitHub, Scratch) but add incentives/badges for _significant_ modifications rather than superficial tweaks.

## 3. Market Design

### Pattern: Innovation-Friendly Friction

**Use when:** Your marketplace is clearing too fast (congestion) or matching purely on price.

- **Action:** Slow down the matching process just enough to allow participants to evaluate fit (e.g., portfolio reviews on Upwork, curated galleries).
- **Goal:** Prioritize long-term effectiveness (high-utility matches) over short-term efficiency.

### Pattern: Strategic Curation

**Use when:** You need to fill gaps in your ecosystem portfolio.

- **Action:** Use your platform's promotion privileges to highlight "up and coming" solutions in neglected categories.
- **Goal:** Direct the "distributed search" of your market toward strategic needs without top-down mandates.

### Pattern: Data Rents as Win-Win

**Use when:** You have privileged access to ecosystem-wide data.

- **Action:** Transform digital footprints into "assetized data" (e.g., trend reports).
- **Goal:** Capture value through data advantage while empowering users with insights they couldn't get alone.
